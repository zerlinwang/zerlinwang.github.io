Having dreams is what makes life tolerable.

Hi there! I am Zilin, a Computer Science DPhil student at the University of Oxford in the British Open-ended Learning and Discovery (BOLD) (previously WhiRL and FLAIR labs). I am fortunate to be co-supervised by Professor Shimon Whiteson and Professor Jakob Foerster. I previously received a Master’s degree from Tsinghua University, supervised by Prof. Zhiyong Wu. Before that, I did my undergraduate studies at Dalian University of Technology.

My research focuses on efficient deep reinforcement learning (RL) and its application in autonomous driving.

In my spare time, I enjoy running, hiking, badminton, fitness, anime, and video games. Additionally, I am a polyglot; apart from my mother tongue Mandarin, I also speak English and Japanese. I love learning new languages because I believe they're like gates to new worlds!

Papers

NeurIPS 2026

Learning to Drive in New Cities Without Human Demonstrations

Zilin Wang, Saeed Rahmani, Daphne Cornelisse, Bidipta Sarkar, Alexander D. Goldie, Jakob N. Foerster, Shimon Whiteson

We show that self-play multi-agent reinforcement learning can adapt a driving policy to a substantially different target city using only the map and meta-information, without requiring any human demonstrations from that city.

NeurIPS 2026 Position

Beyond One-Size-Fits-All: Diagnosis-Driven Online Reinforcement Learning with Offline Priors

Guozheng Ma, Lu Li, Zilin Wang, Pierre-Luc Bacon, Dacheng Tao

NeurIPS 2026 Position Paper Track

Offline priors help online RL but introduce bounded knowledge whose validity varies across deployments. We argue the field should shift from seeking universal methods to diagnosis-driven tension management.

ICML 2026

Procedural Generation Of Algorithm Discovery Tasks in Machine Learning

Alexander D. Goldie, Zilin Wang, Adrian Hayler, Deepak Nathani, Edan Toledo, Ken Thampiratwong, Aleksandra Kalisz, Michael Beukman, Alistair Letcher, Shashank Reddy, Clarisse Wibault, Theo Wolf, Charles O’Neill, Shimon Whiteson, Jakob N. Foerster, Roberta Raileanu

We introduce DiscoGen, a procedural generator of algorithm discovery tasks from different fields in machine learning, which can be used for optimisation of algorithm discovery agents.

RLC 2025

How Should We Meta-Learn Reinforcement Learning Algorithms?

Alexander D. Goldie, Zilin Wang, Jaron Cohen, Jakob N. Foerster, Shimon Whiteson

RLC 2025 Outstanding Paper for Scientific Understanding In Reinforcement Learning

We empirically compares various meta-learning algorithms applied to reinforcement learning components, providing comprehensive guidelines on their trade-offs in performance, computational cost, and interpretability.

ICML 2025

Network Sparsity Unlocks the Scaling Potential of Deep Reinforcement Learning

Guozheng Ma, Lu Li, Zilin Wang, Li Shen, Pierre-Luc Bacon, Dacheng Tao

ICML 2025 Oral presentation (Top ~1%)

Integrating network sparsity into the most advanced architectures can further unlock the scaling potential of DRL models and meanwhile effectively mitigating optimization pathologies during scaling.
